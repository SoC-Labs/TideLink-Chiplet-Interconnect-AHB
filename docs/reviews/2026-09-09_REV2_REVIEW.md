## 0. Verdict

TideLink works on the vehicle it has been validated on: one KR260 with both dies in fabric delivers credit-gated packets byte-exact at 5000/5000 with autonomous bring-up. It is not tapeout-ready, its verification apparatus cannot currently say whether it is, and the file set that would tape out is not the file set that was validated. The in-flight `rev2/integration` branch is a month of real progress on exactly the right problems (coverage, false-green diagnostics, the two AHB-sub wedges) and is itself not yet land-ready. The next iteration should be a consolidation-and-hardening release, not a feature release, with a throughput programme that starts from a measurement and a single named lever.

| Area | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Tapeout** (Critical) | **Not ready.** The ASIC flist compiles the AXI flow-control state machines from `deps/` with zero recovery hooks and CRC ON; the FPGA-validated set has 73 hooks and CRC OFF. The ASIC arm was *measured* wedging at state 7 where the FPGA twin escapes. Two Critical AHB-sub wedges (write hazard-list saturation, read dead gate) are fixed or contained only on rev2, in simulation. No post-constraint-fix timing build is archived; the fix commit says the correctly-constrained design fails timing. DFT is a scaffold. |
| **Verification** (Critical) | **Nothing is gated.** No CI on either forge; the aggregate gate rewrites tracked files and refuses its own PASS; 43 of 63 benches are wired into nothing and at least nine of those are red or unknown. The shipping read and burst datapath has never been simulated as a system. FSM transition coverage is 41.8%. There are zero SystemVerilog assertions and no formal. |
| **Maintainability** (High) | **The "override" directory is the product.** 38k lines of hand-forked vendor Wlink RTL, including six near-identical FCSM copies patched by hand; a 3,477-line top (3,968 on rev2) with a 620-line backstop block that cannot be unit-tested; three inconsistent parameter-default sets; two hand-maintained 186-file flists with ten forks; four descriptions of one register map that disagree; V1 still the default in three entry points; 22 stale claims in the entry docs. |
| **Throughput** (High) | **About 8% of the raw pad rate, and one lever explains it.** The mailbox path costs 3.06 link cycles per 32-bit word at every clock ratio ever measured because the replay window is 16 packets against an ACK round trip of ~49 link cycles. Fixing that plus 8 lanes gives ~3×; multi-word packets on top give ~10× on the same PHY. The 487.9 vs 95.8 cy/word gap was a clock-ratio artefact, not hardware. |
| **rev2/integration** (OK) | **Real, and not land-ready.** ~16 false-green checkers fixed with must-fail controls, the first coverage baseline, ASIC-fileset simulation and an FPGA image built from the tapeout set, TL-042/TL-044 backstops with mutant tests. But it fails its own new flist-divergence gate, the AR/R self-heal does not reach the ASIC flist, one fix was dropped at merge, the checker controls are wired into nothing, and no rev2 RTL has run on hardware. | **Decision outstanding** (Critical) | **A board credential is in public git history** (24 hits, 18 files, since 07-31, beside the username and both board IPs). `rev2/hygiene` stops it going forward. Rotation is the only remediation and it is a decision, not a task. |

## 2. Method and coordination

Eight reviewers worked in parallel against the read-only checkout of origin/main, each with a distinct scope (architecture, transaction-layer RTL, link/PHY/FCSM/PTP RTL, verification, open-defect register, throughput, flow/tooling/hygiene, the rev2 delta). Every finding carries a severity, a confidence grade (verified in code with a line cite, plausible, or hypothesis), an effort estimate and, where possible, a one-line proof recipe. Memory from prior sessions was treated as hints only; each reviewer re-verified against the tree and re-cited line numbers at `5e8bdb5a` (past sessions were burned citing line numbers from patched files). The peer session that authored rev2 was consulted throughout; its findings were folded in with attribution and independently spot-checked (13 of 13 checkable claims verified, two partially, one cannot be gated because the bench ends in `assert True`). One reviewer's dry run wrote gitignored result files into the review tree; they are listed for cleanup. No simulation, synthesis, board or IP-library write was performed. The eight full reviews (~2,500 lines) are attached as an appendix.

## 3. Architecture and structure

TideLink is an AHB-to-AHB chiplet bridge: an AHB subordinate port feeds Arm's XHB500 AHB-to-AXI bridge, five AXI channels become five Wlink flow-control nodes, a sixth node carries the TideLink mailbox/FIFO protocol, and a 16:1 SDR GPIO PHY serialises 128-bit link words over up to eight lanes. The design is sound in outline. Its structure is not: the repository has grown into a fork of its vendor stack plus a monolithic top that carries two years of fix-on-fix backstops as a lab notebook.

### 3.2 Clocks, resets and where the crossings are

| Clock | Source | Consumers | Crossing mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|

Role lock is a mutual clock enable: `wlink_por_reset = ~poresetn | ~role_locked` gates the forwarded pad clock, which is the peer's RX clock. The a2l ACK-pointer crossing (the TL-027 mechanism) is a ping-pong mailbox that cannot tear; the failure is a dropped update, and continuous `w_inc=1` is the correct latest-value discipline for a level, not a band-aid. The AXI nodes' mailboxes still use the deps copies without the coherent-reset fix.

### 3.4 Link, PHY, FCSM and PTP layer

- **The "unreviewed" WIP commit on main splits cleanly.** Its five FCSM edits are FPGA-only (the ASIC flist takes deps); its 68 controller lines are ten `mark_debug` alias wires plus a 14-flop sink-less counter that synthesis will sweep. Part-A of the watchdog (progress proxy) is correct and sim-proven. **Part-B (forced state-7 exit) has a real defect**: it exits without clearing `sop`, leaving a stale NACK on the TX router that re-serialises when grants resume. One-line fix in five files; make Part-B opt-in as the consolidated worktree did, and do not take that worktree's controller (it predates Hazard-3).
- **The five AXI FCSM copies are parameter-only clones** (widths, IDs, replay address width). One parameterised module is feasible but needs ~5 sub-modules parameterised too; adding the `socl_` features once in the Chisel source and regenerating is the better route.
- **Replay nodes have no timeout anywhere**; a silent peer fills the 8-deep AW window and drops `awready` forever (the TL-042 H1 hypothesis stands).
- **PTP servo** cannot converge from an initial offset over one second (the phase-step flag is never cleared, seconds are never corrected, the nanosecond subtraction has no borrow), has no FSM timeouts, an untagged three-word mailbox, un-reset multiplier control flops, a probable stale-timestamp race against the PHC capture CDC, and an AHB port that can stall the bus indefinitely. The mailbox-RO defect from August is fixed on main. Nothing about this servo has run on two boards.
- **The link-clock divider RTL is sound** (glitch-free two-leg mux, three-stage ratio CDC), but its new top port is connected by neither the DFT wrapper nor the Vivado wrapper, there is no SDC for the divided leg or the negedge interlock flops, and the constraints file its header cites does not exist. Ratio-dependent timers (watchdog thresholds, hold cycles, autoneg budget) are undocumented, and asymmetric ratios between dies were never exercised.
- **Bring-up one-shots** that never re-arm: `calibrated_once` (a force-recal door now exists, efficacy unmeasured), the auto-anchor done latch, the retire latch, and the sticky CRC-seen gate on node 6. Autoneg's FIN states deliberately have no timeout and depend on the winscan FSM's timeout with no assertion binding the two; `ST_ERROR` is terminal.
- **Header ECC is live in the V2 tapeout flist** (the local syndrome file); the V1 list would ship the bypass. Link CRC resets OFF on the FPGA AXI nodes and ON on the ASIC ones, with node 6 ON in both; the two combinations have never been validated together and CRC-enable on the two-board pair once caused a both-die NACK wedge.

## 6. Throughput

Yes, there is much that can be done, and it is one thing first. Every simulation throughput number ever taken on the mailbox path collapses onto a single invariant when expressed in link-layer cycles: about 3.06 link cycles per 32-bit word at every clock ratio, because the sender may have only 16 packets un-acknowledged while the acknowledgement round trip is about 49 link cycles. Lane count barely moves it because serialisation hides inside that window stall. The 487.9 versus 95.8 cycles-per-word gap between hardware and simulation is the same 3.06 cycles at an 8:1 instead of a 2:1 clock ratio, plus the live credit-return loop; it contains no PS-to-PL cost at all.

### 6.1 The model, reconciled with every measurement

| Run | hclk / word | link cycles / word | implied ACK RTT (link cycles) | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| sim, link = 6.4 hclk, ample credit | 19.25 | 3.01 | 48.1 | memory 08-01 |
| sim, link = 32 hclk, 4 lanes / 8 lanes | 97.88 / 97.67 | 3.06 / 3.05 | 48.9 / 48.8 | link-survey log |
| sim, link = 128 hclk (KR260 ratio), 4 / 8 lanes | 417.1 / 391.6 | 3.26 / 3.06 | 52.1 / 49.0 | memory 08-01 |
| sim, ack spacing 7 → 0 | 89.1 | 2.78 | 44.5 (fixed pipeline floor) | memory 07-31 |
| sim, live credit loop | 603.4 | 4.71 | 75.4 | memory 08-01 |
| **KR260 hardware**, live credit, ack 7 / 4 | 487.9 / 456.6 | 3.81 / 3.57 | 61.0 / 57.1 | 07-31 HW run |

Units that prior analyses got wrong: one link-layer cycle is 16 UI and carries one 128-bit link word (8 lanes × 16 bits); a TideLink data packet is 13 bytes (4-byte header with ECC, 7-byte payload including a packet-number byte, 2-byte CRC), so one 32-bit word costs one link cycle at 8 lanes and two at 4 lanes. Only four of every sixteen link-word bytes are payload. The KR260 link cycle is 128 hclk (25 MHz hclk, 3.125 MHz UI after the ÷8 carry-over); the 100 MHz ASIC target is 16 hclk.

### 6.4 Verdict

Today the mailbox path delivers 1.6–2.0 Mb/s on KR260 (6–8% of the 25 Mb/s raw pad rate) and, at the same link-cycle cost, about 65 Mb/s on a 100 MHz-UI ASIC (8% of 800 Mb/s). The bus path is roughly 4 Mb/s on the ASIC and cannot be sped up by bursts until a corruption and a wedge are fixed. Next iteration on the same GPIO PHY and pads: window ≥ RTT plus 8 lanes gives one link cycle per word (200 Mb/s ASIC, 25% of raw); multi-word packets on top give about 640 Mb/s (80% of raw), roughly ten times today, all in the digital stack. A faster or DDR link clock multiplies that by two to four. Beyond about 1.6 Gb/s the eight-lane GPIO PHY is the wall and a serial PHY is a different chiplet.

### 6.5 Measure before changing anything

- **Add a link-clock-domain counter** to `tidelink_fcemit_obs` (already an editable override in the tx_link_clk domain): free-running link cycles, per-channel packet count, window-full cycles, ACKs received; snapshot-frozen and 2FF'd to APB like the existing words. Then link cycles per word is read directly on silicon, independent of hclk and host timing. Nothing readable today counts link cycles.
- **Sim, no RTL change**: (1) the lane-mask throughput test at three clock ratios must show ≈3.0 link cycles per word flat; (2) timestamp one packet through the ten hops to decompose the ≈49-cycle RTT; (3) force the credit maxima to emulate windows of 4, 8 and 12 and confirm cycles per word follow RTT/W (12.2, 6.1, 4.1) before committing to the regeneration; (4) time one `ahb_sub` write and read with `hprot[2]` 0 and 1; (5) release threshold 0 vs 20 with pre-seeded credit.
- **KR260, on-fabric counters only**: window-full fraction should read ≈2/3 and link utilisation ≈1/3 at 8 lanes; then one-variable A/B pairs (ACK spacing, release threshold, lane mask, divider) with ≥3 runs each. Verify each counter can read both zero and non-zero on a known stimulus before trusting it.

### 9.1 Rules for every agent

- **One worktree per lane, never the primary, never a worktree that is running a gate** (editing gate tooling dirties the stamps of the run in progress).
- **Every RTL fix ships four arms**: RED (pristine fails), GREEN, MUTANT (detection kept, action removed, must fail), SAFETY (the protection it touches still works and a normal transaction lands after the arm fires). A passing escape test is not a safety test.
- **Every diagnostic ships with a must-fail control** before anyone trusts it; a checker control that is wired into nothing is decoration.
- **Hardware claims need the artefact**: manifest with a clean stamp, autonomy log, soak log, obs-word readback; the vehicle named (on-chip pair, eth-pair, ASIC-fileset image). A subagent once fabricated a full deploy; the coordinator checks the files.
- **Provenance**: source `set_env.sh` and never redirect its output; verify `git submodule status` has no `-` prefix; parse `.status` files, never the aggregate exit code; keep void logs labelled as void.
- **Boards**: `lease show` first, `acquire` alone, then work; never reload PL on a live link; never `reboot` a KR260; POR via `kpor`; never hand-drive the 0x208 triplet; the `0x8403_xxxx` window is undecoded on the eth-chiplet image.
- **Vendor IP**: never write under `/research/AAA/**`; copy into `local_overrides` and document the deviation.
- **Reachability claims finish at the hop that could refute them**; two independent constructions for any root cause that will drive a netlist change.
- **Coordinator**: one agent owns the ledger of open items and their rev2/main status; every lane reports done only with the proof named in its tile.
